The decision

Should Ascend IQ ship?

Decision: HOLD. Hold the top-50 launch of Ascend IQ and approve the $150,000 online eval plan: 7 of 20 test answers put a wrong or unconfirmed fact in front of a VP, against a bar of 0 we set before reading the logs.

Module 6
Ship-or-hold memo
The arguments

1. Client trust and renewals: wrong facts would reach VP board decks under Ascend's name. Evidence: 7 of 20 answers fail a P0 factual gate (3 overclaims, 3 contradict the source, 1 stale price).
2. We can't catch these failures reliably yet (bar item added after reading the logs). Evidence: none of our 5 P0 and P1 risks has Strong coverage (a code gate or two independent checks) today, and the golden set has 20 cases against a floor of 30.
3. Unverified sources reach client answers (bar item added after reading the logs). Evidence: the only recorded agent run, 1 of 1, cited an unverified web source in a client answer. We can't measure the rate until all 20 golden-set questions have recorded runs.

Module 1
Evaluation strategy
User promise

For VP-level strategists at our top enterprise accounts, Ascend IQ promises to answer plain-language market questions in under 10 seconds (P95), with every figure traceable to Ascend’s verified data, or to say clearly when it doesn’t know, so strategists can build board-ready comparisons in minutes instead of hours, and keep making decisions on Ascend data and renew.

Three trust metrics

Hallucination Rate
Latency
Robustness
Why: Hallucination Rate (0 invented figures in the test set): protects "verified." One invented figure in a board deck breaks the reason clients pay.

Trade-offs

Hallucination Rate ↔ Latency: We prioritize hallucination rate over latency because a slower verified answer costs a client a few seconds, while a fast wrong answer costs us the account.
Latency ↔ Robustness: We prioritize robustness over latency because checking whether a question is in scope, and breaking multi-part questions into steps, adds seconds. A strategist will wait a moment for “Ascend doesn’t cover that market” but won’t forgive a confident guess.

Module 3
Eval suite and spec
What the checks caught

Rule check (certainty words): caught 1 of 3 overclaim rows (missed rows 11, 14); no false alarms.
Safety rule (status terms such as tentative, partner-only): caught 2 of 3 (missed row 14); no false alarms.
LLM judge (Gemini): caught 3 of 3; no false alarms; also caught 10 failures of other modes (rows 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12, 16, 19); agreed with my audit on 20 of 20.
Where it was caught: only the judge caught every overclaim. Ascend IQ overclaims by dropping qualifiers and widening scope, not by using certainty words, so word-based rules miss part of it.
What I'd ship next: the judge as the overclaim gate in staging, plus the safety rule as a cheap check on every change. Next fix: make the judge return the failure mode, so frequency is counted by mode instead of by tag.

Eval spec

Target risk · Confident overclaims (#HALLUCINATION, output risk): answers state partial, tentative, partner-only or regional facts as confirmed, or wider than the source. Seen in 3 of 20 golden-set answers (rows 4, 11, 14). Root cause: generation (the answer goes beyond what was retrieved), not retrieval.
Evaluator · Two layers plus human review. (1) Safety rule, code, runs on every pull request against the 20-row golden set and blocks the merge. Status terms: partner-only, partner, unconfirmed, tentative, invited, beta, planned, pending, not public (case-insensitive). If the reference text contains a status term next to a fact, the answer passes only if the same sentence names that fact with the same term or a synonym from the list, or uses a decline phrase ("can't confirm", "not confirmed", "not public"). Returns 1 or 0. The golden set holds the reference as free text today; adding a structured status field per fact is the next step. (2) LLM judge, staging, on the same 20 rows, from a different company than Ascend IQ's model, blocks the release: fails any claim more certain or broader than the reference, any dropped qualifier, or any detail not in the reference; returns 1 or 0 plus one line of reasoning. (3) Human: the PdM reviews every row the judge fails as an overclaim or a contradiction of the reference (the two P0 failure modes) before release.
Threshold · 0 overclaim fails on the golden set, and the safety rule passes 100%. Supported by Lab 1: the judge caught 3 of 3 overclaim rows and agreed with my audit on 20 of 20, so a zero-tolerance line can be enforced without false fails. The current beta fails it (3 of 20).
Business stakes · VPs take wrong competitive positions to their boards on Ascend's word. That breaks the reason clients pay for verified intelligence and puts renewals across the top 50 accounts at risk: at least $2.5M a year (50 accounts x $50K minimum).
Owner · GPdM for Ascend IQ (me) owns the threshold and the ship sign-off. The engineering lead owns both gates in the pipeline. Fixes go to the prompt/generation team.

Release criteria

Hard · Confident overclaims (P0): 0 overclaim fails and a 100% safety-rule pass. LLM judge at Staging, safety rule at Pull Request, rerun at Release as a kill switch. No override.
Soft · Latency: P95 ≤ 10 s (my M1 bar, set before reading the logs). Timer at Staging. A fail pauses release until the PdM signs off.
Advisory · Off-brand tone (P3): 0 slang hits. Word list at Pull Request; a hit posts a warning only.
If the Soft gate fails: staged rollout to 5 of the top 50 accounts, with their account teams briefed. Roll back if live P95 stays above 10 s or those accounts use Ascend IQ less than platform search.
Call today: delay launch. The beta fails every Hard gate it has been tested on: overclaims 3 of 20, contradicts the reference 3 of 20, stale data 1 of 20, unverified source on 1 of 1 recorded run.

Module 5
Coverage and budget
Coverage gaps

AI Report Summaries · ✅ Covered · ❌ Gap · ⚠️ Partial · ❌ Gap · ❌ Gap
Accepted gap: Robustness. We accept this gap because AI Report Summaries only takes in Ascend's own research reports, a format our team controls, not open client questions. The release judge already scores every report type we ship today, so a separate robustness suite would cost more than the risk it removes right now. We’ll look again: If a new report format or data source launches, or if one client flags a summary that missed the report's main point. Otherwise, at the next quarterly eval budget review.
Gap to close first: We've flagged UX Trust as the most critical gap for AI Report Summaries because summaries go straight to client VPs with nobody reviewing them first. One casual or alarmist line reaches a $50K+ account under Ascend's name, the client's account lead hears about it first, and that renewal is at risk. To close it, we'll add a tone eval gate before every release. It reuses the Competitor Signal Tracker's existing style check (it flags casual or alarmist tone), adds a tone line to the release judge that already runs, and scores every summary in the release test set against the brand-voice guide. Any fail blocks the release.

Why Overconfident Tone is online: it is my Confident overclaims, a P0 with a Hard gate in Module 4, not the lab's P1.
Periodic audit · Context Specificity (Robustness · P1), the required P0/P1 downgrade: every answer restates the filters it applied, and every week a reviewer scores 50 live answers from filtered, date-bound or multi-part questions; reruns on every prompt or retrieval change. Enough because a dropped filter becomes visible before the number reaches a deck. Misses individual misses between reviews.
Periodic audit · Data Bias (Fairness · P2): every two weeks, a reviewer compares matched US and APAC answers for depth and number of sources. Enough because bias only shows across many answers. Moves online on the first gap found.
Code-based check · Slow Answers (Latency · P3): a timer on every request, alerting on rolling P95 over 10 s, on P99, and on P95 per top-50 account. Enough because it catches every slow answer at 5% of the online cost. Misses why an answer was slow; traces cover that.

Insights

What I learned

Where the course was hardest, what you’ll keep doing, and the moment your thinking changed.

Friction

Holding the line when the AI reviewer pushed back with invented numbers (an unsourced 'Q4 renewal ARR', a '$50K conditional eval', '10/20'). I checked every number against the repo before accepting a change.

Key learnings

- Write the bar before reading the results, and label anything added after. It's the difference between a standard and a rationalization.
- Count failures by mode, not by tag. One stale-price row had been riding an 11-of-20 tag count.
- Never let a judge grade its own company's model or work it helped draft.

Aha moment

A certainty-word rule caught 1 of 3 overclaims. Ascend IQ doesn't say 'definitely'; it quietly drops 'tentative'. You only catch what you define precisely.
